The problem

Theatre runs on spreadsheets, memory,
and a group chat.

Every theatre organization we've met stitches together the same patchwork of disconnected tools, and the seams are where money and momentum leak out.

01

The budget surprise

A production closes in the red because nobody modeled break-even against the run length until closing night was already behind it.

02

The board flying blind

Trustees see a PDF once a quarter instead of a live number, so risk gets discovered in the annual meeting instead of the month it happened.

03

The disconnected donor

Development runs in a separate CRM from the production calendar, so nobody can say which show or campaign actually moved a gift.

04

The tribal knowledge

Cue sheets, prop lists, and rehearsal schedules live in someone's inbox, one departure away from being lost entirely.

The solution

One model, every department.

Banquo isn't a bundle of separate tools wearing one logo. Every module reads from and writes to the same underlying production and financial data.

Why one data model wins

Compute it once. See it everywhere.

When a production's ticket revenue or expenses change, the same break-even calculation updates a warning badge on the Financial Dashboard, a status inside the Production Budget Manager, and a "Productions at Risk" card on the Board Dashboard, automatically, from one shared service.

No re-entry. No reconciliation. No version of the truth that's two weeks stale. That's the architecture bet competitors built as separate point tools can't easily retrofit.
